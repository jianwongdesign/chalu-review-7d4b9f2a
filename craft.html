Give good tea time.

Chalu uses a cold infusion followed by time on native Yunnan oak. The process gives the tea a central role in the finished drink.

You can explore that character in the glass. Begin with the aroma, take a small sip, then notice what remains after the bubbles settle. A second sip with food may draw your attention to a different part of the tea.

Keep the serving simple for your first taste. A clear glass and a well-chilled bottle let you begin with the tea itself.

How to serve Chalu ↗

Read the glass, one sip at a time.

The tasting notes on each product page describe the experience of drinking the tea. A note of toasted nuts, for example, describes an aroma; the ingredient information is listed separately so you can see what is in the blend.

Try comparing two expressions side by side. Give each a clean glass, take your time with the first sip and revisit it after the other. You may find yourself noticing the finish more clearly on the second round.

Aroma

Before tasting, bring the glass close and take a gentle breath. Use the botanical notes as a starting point, then describe the aroma in your own words.

Texture & finish

Notice the bubbles as you sip and the character that stays afterwards. Taste again with a little food to explore how the combination changes your impression.

Four teas. A world of character.

Bring what you have discovered into the glass. Compare the four expressions, explore their tasting notes and choose a bottle for the food you enjoy sharing.

Every tea has its own page with the blend, serving guidance and suggested dishes to try.
