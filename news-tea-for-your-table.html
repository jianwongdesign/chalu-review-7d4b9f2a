Choose a tea for your next meal.

Start with the food you love, then explore a tea alongside it. A practical guide to planning your next pour.

Sample editorial story for this design preview.

Let one dish lead

Think about the main thing you are cooking before choosing a bottle. A light lunch, a roast dinner and a fruit dessert each give you a different place to begin.

The collection page groups the teas by the kind of meal you might enjoy with them. Browse a few options, then open an individual tea’s page for a closer look at its tasting notes.

Try the suggested pairings

For a lighter meal, explore Snow Jasmine with steamed fish or fresh rice-paper rolls. For something richer, try Hibiscus Noir alongside roast duck or a soft cheese board.

Volcano Oolong is worth exploring with grilled fish or roasted mushrooms. For a fresh lunch, consider Untamed Rosé with salmon, or tomato and burrata. These are editorial suggestions to try at home, rather than a fixed set of rules.

Taste before and after a bite

Pour a little tea before serving the food. Once you have tasted it on its own, take another sip after a bite of the dish. Notice what you enjoy about the combination and what you might change next time.

If you are serving several courses, keep some tea to revisit later in the meal. You may prefer a different pairing to the one you expected. Make a note of the combinations you would happily serve again.
